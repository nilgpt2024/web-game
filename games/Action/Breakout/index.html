Breakout — Game Introduction

Breakout is the original brick-breaker: a paddle at the bottom, a ball bouncing around the screen, and a wall of bricks that has to disappear. Every brick you clear scores points; every ball you drop costs a life. The ball gets quicker as the wall thins, so the end of a level is always harder than the start.

This version keeps the 1976 geometry and adds levelled walls plus a running score, turning a single-screen arcade game into a run you can build on. The skill ceiling is not reflexes alone — it is controlling the angle of return, because the paddle edge sends the ball sideways while the centre sends it almost straight up.

How to Play Breakout

Move the paddle left and right to keep the ball in play. The ball bounces off the side walls, the top edge and the bricks. Bricks that are hit break and add to the score; clearing the wall advances you to the next level, where the layout and the speed change.

If the ball passes the paddle you lose a life, and the run ends when the last life is gone. Because the ball accelerates through a level, the second half of every wall is a catching test rather than an aiming one.

Controls

  • Mouse — move the paddle horizontally
  • Left and right arrows — keyboard steering
  • Touch drag — finger steering on mobile
  • Start / serve — release the ball to begin a rally

Strategy & Tips

  1. Hit with the paddle edge to change angle. Centre hits return the ball almost vertically, which keeps it in the same column and stalls progress.
  2. Aim for the gaps. Once a tunnel forms in the wall, the ball spends more time above it and clears bricks faster than side-to-side play.
  3. Keep the ball high. The longer it stays in the brick field, the more it scores before coming back to you.
  4. Do not chase the ball. Position the paddle where the ball will land, not where it currently is.
  5. Treat the last few bricks as the hardest phase. With fewer targets the ball returns faster, so shorten your paddle movements.

Background

Breakout grew out of Atari's 1976 arcade cabinet, itself a refinement of an earlier paddle-and-ball game, and became the template for an entire genre: Arkanoid, Alleyway and countless browser remakes all reuse its geometry. The design is unusually pure — one moving wall, one ball, one line of defence — which is why it is still used to teach collision physics.

FAQ

How does scoring work in Breakout?

Every brick destroyed adds points. Clearing the whole wall moves you to the next level, where bricks are arranged differently and the ball is quicker.

What happens when the ball drops?

Each dropped ball costs one life, and the run ends when the last life is gone.

How do I aim the ball?

Centre hits send it almost straight up; hits near the edge angle it sharply sideways. That difference is the only aiming control in the game.

Can I play with a keyboard only?

Yes. The left and right arrow keys steer the paddle without any mouse input.

Does Breakout work on phones?

Yes. Dragging a finger moves the paddle, and the playfield scales to the viewport.
