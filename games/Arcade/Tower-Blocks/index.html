Tower Blocks — Game Introduction

Tower Blocks is the one-button stacking game that turned a simple reflex test into a genre. A block swings above a growing tower; you release it at the right moment, and whatever hangs over the edge is sliced away. The tower becomes narrower as your timing slips, and the round ends when nothing is left to land on.

What makes it more than a reflex test is the compounding: every block you place becomes the base for the next one, so a single early miss narrows the tower for the rest of the run. The score climbs with each successful landing and the best height is stored locally, so the only pressure is your own last attempt.

How to Play Tower Blocks

Tap, click or press to drop the moving block onto the tower. The overlapping part stays; the overhang is cut off and falls away. Land it dead centre and the full width is kept, which keeps later drops forgiving. Land it near the edge and the tower narrows, making every following drop tighter.

The camera rises as the tower grows, so the pace increases with height. Several centre hits in a row build a visibly taller, more stable tower and are the main route to a large score. The run ends when a block misses the tower entirely.

Controls

  • Tap / click — release the moving block
  • Space bar — the same action on desktop
  • Best score — shown in the header and saved in your browser
  • Restart — begins a fresh tower at any time

Strategy & Tips

  1. Drop on the beat, not on sight. The block's motion is regular; counting the swing rhythm steadies timing better than watching the edge.
  2. Aim for the centre line. The middle of the tower is the widest safe zone and keeps full width for the next drop.
  3. Slow your rebuild after a miss. Once the tower narrows, the correct response is to stabilise width, not to chase a perfect hit.
  4. Keep your hand still between drops. Most misses in this genre come from a second accidental input, not from a bad read.
  5. Remember the best score is local. Clearing browser storage resets it, so treat a big run as practice rather than permanent progress.

Background

The stacker formula spread through mobile app stores in the mid-2010s and became a standard browser arcade warm-up: one input, an endlessly rising camera, and a score that measures nothing but consistency. It is a good example of a game whose depth comes entirely from the consequences of the previous move.

FAQ

How does scoring work in Tower Blocks?

Each block that lands on the tower adds to the score; taller, cleaner stacks accumulate faster because the run survives longer.

What happens when I miss the tower?

The round ends immediately and the final score is shown against the saved best.

Does the tower get harder as it grows?

Yes. The tower narrows with every imperfect drop and the camera rises, so both width and reaction time shrink as you climb.

Is the best score saved between visits?

It is stored in your browser's local storage, so it survives a reload but not clearing site data.

Can I play Tower Blocks on a phone?

Yes. A single tap is the whole control scheme, which suits touch screens.
