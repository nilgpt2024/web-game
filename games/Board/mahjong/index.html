How to play Mahjong Solitaire

Match pairs of identical free tiles to remove them. A tile is free when nothing rests on top of it and either its left or right side is unblocked. Clear all 72 tiles to win.

Strategy

Plan ahead — bad early matches can lock the board. When you see four of a kind exposed, match all four; otherwise, match pairs that unblock the most tiles. Use HINT when stuck, or SHUFFLE if no moves remain.

About Mahjong Solitaire

Mahjong Solitaire is a single-player tile-matching puzzle played with mahjong tiles. Match pairs of identical free tiles to remove them from a stacked layout. Despite the shared name and tiles, this is a different game from full Mahjong (the four-player draw-and-discard game). Mahjong Solitaire was popularized on early personal computers in the 1980s, notably as Shanghai in 1986.

How to play

Tap two matching free tiles to remove them as a pair. A tile is free when (1) no tile sits on top of it AND (2) at least one of its left or right edges has no tile directly beside it. Win by clearing all 72 tiles from the layout. You can lock yourself out by removing tiles in the wrong order, so plan ahead — tiles buried deeper require their cover removed first.

Frequently asked questions

How do you play?

Tap two matching free tiles to remove the pair. Repeat until the board is empty. A tile is free when nothing is on top of it AND its left or right side is open.

What does "free" mean?

(1) No tile sits on top of it. (2) At least one of its left or right edges has no neighbor. A tile blocked on both sides is not selectable until a neighbor is removed.

What tile types are there?

Two suits only: Bamboo (1–9) and Characters (1–9), 4 copies of each of the 18 designs, for 72 tiles total. Tiles pair only with an exactly identical tile — there are no Winds, Dragons, Dots, Flowers, or Seasons in this version.

Are all layouts solvable?

Each new board is reshuffled until at least one opening match exists, so there is always a legal first move — but full solvability is not guaranteed, and a wrong-order removal can still strand tiles. Use Shuffle to re-randomize the remaining tiles or Undo to back out of a dead end.

How is this different from full Mahjong?

Full Mahjong is a four-player turn-based draw-and-discard game similar to Rummy, scored by hand value. Mahjong Solitaire is a single-player tile-matching puzzle. They share the tile set; nothing else.

How this version works

The layout

All 72 tiles sit in a four-layer stepped pyramid. The bottom layer is 8 tiles wide and 4 rows deep (32 tiles). The next layer is 6 by 4 (24 tiles), then 4 by 3 (12 tiles), and the top is a 2 by 2 block of 4 tiles. Every layer sits on the same grid as the one below, so a tile counts as covered only when another tile sits exactly on top of it. Upper layers are drawn shifted slightly up and to the left so you can see the depth. Tiles that are not currently free are dimmed.

How boards are dealt

The 18 designs go into a deck of four copies each, which is shuffled with a Fisher-Yates shuffle using your browser's random number generator and laid onto the pyramid in order, bottom layer first. There is no seed and no daily board; every New press deals a fresh random layout.

Selecting and matching

Click a free tile to select it, click it again to deselect it. If the second tile you click does not match, it simply becomes the new selection. Matching two identical tiles removes both.

Hint, Undo and Shuffle

  • Hint highlights one free matching pair. It scans tiles from the bottom layer up, row by row, so it always points to the first pair it finds rather than the best one. Hints are unlimited and do not affect anything.
  • Undo puts back the last removed pair. You can undo all the way to the start of the board.
  • Shuffle mixes the designs of all remaining tiles without moving their positions. It tries up to 50 times to produce at least one free pair. If none of those attempts works, it tells you no moves are possible and suggests Undo.

When no free pair is left on the board, a message appears suggesting Shuffle or Undo.

What is tracked

There is no score. The counter shows how many pairs are left, and a timer runs until the board is cleared. Nothing is stored in your browser, so reloading the page deals a new board.

How to Play Mahjong Solitaire

Mahjong Solitaire is a single-player tile-matching game — not the four-player gambling game of the same name. You clear a layered layout of tiles by matching identical pairs until the board is empty. The AIGameHub build uses a compact 72-tile set drawn from two mahjong suits: Bamboo and Characters, nine faces each, four copies of every face. It runs in one HTML page with hint, undo, and shuffle.

Free vs blocked tiles

You can only remove a tile that is free. A tile is free when two conditions are both true:

  • Uncovered — nothing rests on top of it in a higher layer.
  • Open edge — its left OR its right side is unblocked. A tile with a neighbor on both its left and right is locked, even if nothing sits on top.

Blocked tiles are dimmed. Freeing them is the whole puzzle: every pair you remove may open the tiles that were leaning on it.

Exact-glyph matching

Two free tiles match only if they show the exact same glyph. In this build there are no flower or season groups — a 3-Bamboo pairs only with another 3-Bamboo, never with a Character tile that merely looks similar. Because every face has four copies, each glyph forms two possible pairs on a full board.

The four-of-a-kind rule: when all four copies of a face are free at once, clear all four immediately. Leaving two behind risks stranding them under later tiles with no partner reachable.

Plan from the top down

The fastest way to lose is matching the first pair you see. A greedy match can bury a tile whose only partner is now locked. Instead:

  1. Clear the top layers first. Upper tiles cover the most cells below them, so removing them opens the widest set of future moves.
  2. Look before you match. If a glyph has all four copies visible but you only need to open one specific tile, match the pair that frees the most blocked tiles.
  3. Keep a spare pair. Avoid clearing both pairs of a face early if one of those tiles is the only thing you will be able to match later.

Hint, undo, and shuffle

Use HINT to highlight a legal pair when you are stuck, and UNDO to step back after a match that locked the board. If no legal moves remain among the free tiles, SHUFFLE reassigns the faces on the remaining tiles so the game can continue. A clean solve uses shuffle sparingly — treat it as a rescue, not a strategy.
