Klotski — Game Introduction

Klotski is the sliding-block puzzle that ranks with the Rubik's Cube as one of the great hand-held brain-teasers. A small board is packed with blocks of different sizes, and one large block — traditionally the warlord Cao Cao — has to reach the exit at the bottom. Nothing can be lifted, rotated or stacked; blocks only slide into empty space, and the empty space is always the bottleneck.

That constraint is the whole design. With one or two free squares on a crowded board, every move consumes the space the next move needs, so progress depends on planning several slides ahead rather than reacting. The classic layout, known as Heng Dao Li Ma ("horizontal sword, immediate horse"), needs dozens of moves even when played optimally.

How to Play Klotski

Slide blocks one square at a time into the empty space. Vertical 1x2 blocks, horizontal 2x1 blocks and single pawns all move differently, and the 2x2 master block is the one that must exit through the gap in the bottom wall.

Clear a route to the exit, then walk the master block down through it. Because the free space is so tight, the reliable method is to work from the exit upwards: open the corridor first, then move the pieces blocking the master block's path, then shift it one step and rebuild the corridor behind it.

Controls

  • Drag — slide a block with the mouse
  • Click plus arrow keys — select a block, then nudge it
  • Touch slide — drag with a finger on mobile
  • Undo — step back move by move
  • Hint — surfaces the next useful move when you are stuck
  • Reset — restores the starting layout

Strategy & Tips

  1. Think in corridors, not blocks. The winning move is always one that keeps a path open from the master block to the exit.
  2. Move the vertical pawns early. They are the cheapest way to free a column and the usual reason a corridor collapses.
  3. Never fill the cell directly below the master block. If that square is occupied, every other move is wasted until it clears.
  4. Count moves. Strong players treat the known optimum as the target rather than merely solving the puzzle, and the in-game counter makes that measurable.
  5. Use undo as a study tool. Reversing one move often reveals that the block you moved was not the one blocking the path.
  6. Learn one layout properly before moving on. Patterns repeat across layouts far more than beginners expect.

Background

Klotski descends from Chinese sliding-block puzzles documented in the early twentieth century and spread worldwide as a wooden toy before arriving on screens. The name comes from a Polish word for "sliding puzzle" rather than from Chinese, a reminder of how quickly the genre crossed borders. Its enduring appeal is structural: a tiny board, a strict rule set, and an enormous search space.

FAQ

How do I win at Klotski?

Slide the 2x2 master block through the exit gap in the bottom wall. Every other block exists only to make that possible.

Can blocks be lifted or rotated?

No. Blocks only translate into adjacent empty squares, which is why the puzzle is so constrained.

What is Heng Dao Li Ma?

It is the best-known classic layout, named after a phrase from the Romance of the Three Kingdoms, and the standard benchmark for move count.

How many moves does the classic layout take?

Dozens. The exact minimum depends on the layout, and the in-game move counter lets you compare your route against it.

Does the game work on mobile?

Yes. Blocks can be dragged with a finger, and the board scales to the screen.

Is Klotski free?

Yes — free to play in the browser, with no download, account or registration.
