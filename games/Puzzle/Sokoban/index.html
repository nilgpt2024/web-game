Sokoban Game Introduction

Sokoban, from 1981 Japan, is one of the classic spatial-planning puzzles. Meaning warehouse keeper, you play the mover shoving crates onto marked goal squares in a cramped warehouse.

The catch: you can only push crates, never pull them. One bad push into a corner often means undo or restart. Every step must be planned mentally — supreme spatial training.

How to Play Sokoban

Walk beside a crate and step toward it to push. Get every crate onto a goal square to clear the level. Crates only push — a wrong move may be unrecoverable, so use undo.

Controls

  • Arrow keys or W A S D: move
  • R: restart the level
  • U / Z: undo one step
  • Touch: on-screen buttons or swipe

Features

  • Levels deepen into full route planning
  • Undo encourages trying different routes
  • Pure logic puzzle — play instantly offline
  • Excellent spatial and sequencing training

Tips & Tricks

  1. Plan each crate's destination first, then work backward.
  2. Avoid pushing crates into walls or jamming them side by side.
  3. Push to a staging spot first, then re-approach from the right direction.
  4. Undo after three bad pushes — cheaper than hitting a dead end.

FAQ

Why can't I pull boxes?

You can only push, never pull — that's the challenge; plan ahead.

Box stuck — what now?

Undo or restart with a different pushing order.
