Hangman — Game Introduction

Hangman is the word-guessing game that has been played with pencil and paper in classrooms for well over a century. A secret word is hidden behind blank slots; every letter you name that belongs to the word is revealed, and every letter that does not builds one more stroke of the drawing. You get six wrong letters before the round is lost, so the whole game is a balance between testing obvious letters and saving your mistakes for the letters you genuinely cannot narrow down.

This browser edition keeps that scoring intact but adds what paper never had: a word database split into categories, a remaining-chances counter, and instant input from either the on-screen keyboard or a physical one. It runs entirely in the page — no account, no download, no install.

How to Play Hangman

Pick a category from the selector, then start a round. The word appears as a row of blanks, one per letter. Guess with the on-screen keyboard or by typing a letter directly.

Correct letters fill every matching blank at once. A wrong letter costs one of your six chances and adds a line to the drawing. When the word is fully revealed the round is won; when the six chances are gone the answer is shown. Repeated letters are counted once, and letters already guessed are ignored, so only new attempts change the board.

Controls

  • On-screen keyboard — click or tap any letter to guess it
  • Physical keyboard — typing a letter registers instantly
  • Category selector — switches the word pool between rounds
  • Chance counter — sits beside the drawing and shows lives left
  • Restart — begins a new word, keeping your saved category

Strategy & Tips

  1. Start with vowels. E, A, I, O and U appear in most short English words, so one guess can unlock several blanks at once.
  2. Use frequency order, not intuition. After the vowels, T, N, S, R, H and L are the next most common letters in everyday vocabulary.
  3. Guess one letter at a time when risk matters. Guessing by habit is fine early; in the last two chances, only name a letter you have a real reason for.
  4. Watch the blank pattern. A four-letter word ending in a single blank, once three letters are known, is a narrow set — think before spending a chance.
  5. Do not repeat failures. The board strikes through rejected letters; rereading it prevents the most common beginner mistake.
  6. Use the category deliberately. If the pool is animals, reach for letters that suit animal names rather than general prose.

Background

The game is usually traced to Victorian England, where it appeared in puzzle books as "Birds, Beasts and Fishes" before the gallows drawing became standard. It survived because the rules fit on a single sheet of paper and need nothing but a pencil — which is also why it remains one of the most common first programming exercises and one of the most played browser word games.

FAQ

How many wrong guesses do I get in Hangman?

Six. Each incorrect letter adds one stroke to the drawing; the seventh mistake ends the round.

Does the game accept physical keyboard input?

Yes. Typing a letter on a real keyboard works exactly like pressing the on-screen button, and both are ignored once a letter has already been guessed.

Is there a time limit?

No. The round waits for you. Only guesses are counted, so you can reason as long as you like.

Does the category choice change difficulty?

It changes the vocabulary pool rather than the rules. Longer, more technical word sets make narrowing down harder, which raises the real difficulty.

Is this Hangman free and does it need an account?

It is free, needs no account or install, and works in any modern mobile or desktop browser.
